Asia Music Eventan international conference connecting Asia's electronic music scenes, held in Hong Kong for the first time

Asia Music Event, an international conference for the electronic music industry and its scene, holds its first edition in Hong Kong on 26 and 27 November, with 100+ speakers, 50+ sessions and a closing party. TAGS: hongkong, conference, asia, news

On Thursday, November 26 and Friday, November 27, Soho House Hong Kong hosts the Asia Music Event (AME), an international conference born in Asia and built for the electronic music industry and its scenes. This first edition brings together over 100 speakers across more than 50 sessions.

An official closing party caps the final night. Techno Japan is on board as an official media partner, bringing coverage of the event to Japan.

What is the Asia Music Event?

Through panels, workshops and community-led programming, AME gathers everyone from major industry players to artists, creators and independent communities in a single space. Its official tagline: "Where the Global Electronic Music Community Meets Asia's Next Wave."

The starting point is the idea of "Built By the Scene, For the Scene." Across Asia, scenes have been cultivating talent and subcultures, yet they have largely developed in parallel, with few opportunities to learn from one another. AME aims to create that space and open a two-way dialogue between East and West.

Why Asia, why Hong Kong?

Asia's music markets look dramatically different from country to country and region to region. Mature markets, fast-emerging audiences and self-contained local scenes sit side by side, each operating under its own culture, business customs and regulations. That diversity is a huge opportunity, but it has also made cross-border expansion difficult.

What AME is after is a relationship in which Asia and the wider world learn from each other. Host city Hong Kong has long been a cultural crossroads, positioned between international business, entertainment and the markets of Asia. The event is also supported by the Hong Kong Tourism Board (HKTB).

Two days, 100-plus speakers

Session formats range widely: panel discussions, one-on-one fireside chats, workshops, masterclasses and live recordings of radio shows and podcasts. The overall design is "Conference by Day, Culture by Night" — conference programming in the daytime, the music itself after dark.

The programme is built on three pillars. "Innovation" covers technology and new business models, including AI, payments and data. "Cross-Regional Collaboration" connects Asia's country-by-country music ecosystems. And "Talent & Culture" shines a light on Asian artists, sounds and movements.

The speakers span generations and regions. Andrew Bull, the Hong Kong DJ and pioneer of the city's nightlife culture. Meindert Kennis, former CEO of Amsterdam Dance Event (ADE), and Gary Smith, who shaped ADE's programming for some three decades. Jim Wong, Managing Director of Live Nation Electronic Asia.

From labels, festivals and media come Kompakt's Michael Mayer, Verknipt, Wonderfruit, DJ Mag, CTM Festival curator and former Boiler Room figure Michail Stangl, and OIL Club's Yangyang Song, among others. Major labels and large-scale festivals line up alongside underground clubs and independent promoters.

Sessions to watch

One highlight is "Staying Independent in a Consolidating Market," where festival operators from Australia's Untitled Group, Mongolia's Playtime Festival and Hong Kong's Kolour Entertainment discuss how independents can hold on to their identity — and keep going — as the industry consolidates.

"Beyond the Showcase: Making Cross Cultural Collaboration Work," moderated by Gary Smith, tackles how to make cross-border collaboration actually function. In "Where Does Asia Stand, and What Will It Take to Lead?", Bloomberg's Tiffany Ap asks Jim Wong what it will take for Asia to move from tour destination to industry driver.

Other topics stretch across both business and culture: China's electronic music market, underground communities, artist development, and ticketing and payment technology. The full programme is available on the official programme page.

The closing party and the city at large

The two days wrap with the official closing party on the night of November 27. It takes place at the same Soho House Hong Kong, co-hosted with local promoter 0159. Headlining are Belgian techno duo Joyhauser and Australian-born, Berlin-based Juliet Fox.

Joyhauser are known for their releases on Terminal M, Juliet Fox for hers on Drumcode and Filth On Acid; both have played Awakenings and Tomorrowland. The same night also features the winner of the AME x Tomorrowland Academy DJ Contest, with Tomorrowland Academy shortlisting three entrants and AME selecting the final winner.

During the conference and in the days around it, partner venues and promoters across the city will stage satellite events. AOAO, Cassio Social Club, Space Club, Upper Hong Kong, Magic Room and Mushroom are among those taking part, connecting international visitors with Hong Kong's local scene.

Techno Japan and AME

Asia's scenes have had few places to get to know one another. That awareness at the heart of AME overlaps with Techno Japan's own stance: to make Japan and the world intersect.

Where is Asian electronic music headed next? We'll be bringing the conversations happening in Hong Kong to readers in Japan.
