DEI FESTHosei University's Tama Campus turns into a techno rave, free to enter

DEI FEST 2026 takes place on 27 November at Hosei University's Tama Campus: a non-profit free rave run by students and young creators, with four acts including RISA TANIGUCHI and Herbalistek.

On Friday, November 27, DEI FEST 2026 takes place at Hosei University's Tama Campus. It's a free-entry rave centered on techno and electronic music, planned and run entirely by students and young creatives as a non-profit free rave.

The lineup features four acts: RISA TANIGUCHI, Herbalistek, Rinalucky, and the organizers themselves, Co-domotachi. The event is scheduled to run from 15:30 to 19:30.

What is DEI FEST?

The name "DEI" stands for Diversity, Equity and Inclusion. The project began from the idea that music can be a catalyst for sharing the same space and experience across differences in nationality, cultural background, social position and values.

At last year's edition, DJs and students from a wide range of roots and backgrounds took part as performers, organizing staff and attendees. The initiative even drew a message of support from the President of Hosei University. This year carries forward the same philosophy, aiming to create a space open to anyone, regardless of their musical experience.

Why a free rave?

Music has become something easily enjoyed through streaming, social media and commercial events. At the same time, it has become easier to evaluate and consume it in terms of short-term buzz and commercial value.

Historically, techno and rave culture have kept their distance from existing social norms and commercial frameworks, exploring free expression and alternative forms of community. DEI FEST is an attempt to put that spirit into practice on a university campus today.

Charging no admission is a deliberate choice to avoid narrowing access based on financial circumstances. The emphasis is on performers, organizing staff and attendees not being divided into providers and consumers, but all becoming participants who build the space together.

The organizing team positions the event as an alternative option standing alongside commercial ways of enjoying music.

Why hold it on a university campus?

A university is, by nature, a place where people with different knowledge, values and cultural backgrounds gather and interact. The organizing team sees that character as resonating with rave culture's emphasis on free expression and community. Spaces normally used for study and research are transformed into sites of exchange and expression through music.

By staging it somewhere close at hand, the event creates an opportunity for students who have never had the chance to go to a club to encounter serious electronic music. Having students plan the event themselves and collaborate with professional artists and production crews is another of its aims.

Artists and sound system

Of the four acts on the bill, RISA TANIGUCHI, Herbalistek and Rinalucky are all artists active in Japan's club scene. Herbalistek has also appeared at this autumn's Sawagi Festival and Intergalactic Festival. Co-domotachi perform as the event's organizers.

For sound, the plan is to bring in a TW AUDIO system of the kind used in professional settings. Despite free admission, the organizers insist on making no compromises on the quality of sound and space. The sound, lighting and spatial production are all made possible by sponsorship and cooperation from people they have met through their activities so far.

Rather than leaving it as a one-off, the organizing team intends to grow the project into an ongoing platform through which students and young creatives can keep putting out music and culture.
