FEATURE: YUTAFrom trance to techno. Liquid Drop Groove, ten years on.

He started out in 2004 as a Goa trance DJ before shifting to techno in 2015. Liquid Drop Groove, the label he launched the following year, marks its tenth anniversary this year. YUTA talks about DJ intuition, the label's three series, and the relaunch of the open-air festival EDEN.

In 2004, he made his DJ debut at a club in Tokyo. He spent his twenties as part of MATSURI DIGITAL, a leading Goa trance label, before shifting to techno in 2015. Liquid Drop Groove, which he launched the following year in 2016, runs on two wheels — an event series and a label — and marks its 10th anniversary this year. What does a DJ who came from trance bring to techno, and where does he go next? We asked YUTA.

ORIGIN

From trance to techno.

It started with "MOON," a mix CD released by Goa trance DJ DOMINO. Shiva Chandra, Goa Gil, Astral Projection, and TIP and Dragonfly — labels whose names are etched into the history of Goa trance. Drawn deep into the Goa trance of the '90s, he began collecting CDs and records.

  1. 2002DOMINO's mix CD "MOON" is released; he picks it up later
  2. 2003Acquires a Pioneer CDJ100 and DJM300
  3. 2004Makes his DJ debut at a club in Tokyo
  4. 2009Becomes part of leading Goa trance label "MATSURI DIGITAL"
  5. 2015Shifts to techno
  6. 2016Founds Liquid Drop Groove

At MATSURI DIGITAL he played a role in the global Goa trance movement, learning label operations and getting involved in production while still in his twenties. His sets at the time were anchored in Goa trance but folded in late-'80s to early-'90s EBM and acid techno, early-2000s progressive and tech trance, and darker sounds from Russia and Australia.

After 2010 a new-school Goa trance movement emerged, but it never made it into YUTA's sets. During a stretch of days when he couldn't find new music, he returned to a Cio D'Or mix he had been given at 26 and began listening again several years later. That was the trigger: slowly, his ear began to change.

Today his activity centres on his work as an artist — DJing and production. Until the pandemic, the producer side was more prominent, but it is those two wheels together that shape Liquid Drop Groove and YUTA as they are now.

PSYCHEDELIC

What remained

The psychedelic sensibility he cultivated in the trance scene still breathes inside his techno today. It shows in his trippy DJ style, in selections that run deep, driving and at times housey, and in sets structured to cut across a range of elements.

INTUITION

Built on the spot.

Not a flow decided in advance, but something constructed from instinct in the moment, reading the air of the floor in real time. YUTA names it as one of the most important skills a DJ can have.

His floor-reading has been built up by playing everywhere, from the tiniest spaces to large-scale venues. And sometimes that instinct overtakes the plan.

LIQUID DROP GROOVE

Where it stands in year ten.

Liquid Drop Groove (LDG), launched in 2016, marks its 10th anniversary this year. Running an event series and a label in tandem, it has doggedly kept putting out both digital and vinyl releases. After those ten years, what lies ahead is overseas shows and the relaunch of the outdoor festival.

CATALOGUE: ALPHA / BETA / DELTA

The LDG catalogue is split into three series. The concept goes back ten years, to when the label was founded.

So instead, he set a theme for each.

  • ALPHA: overseas artists
  • DELTA: domestic artists
  • BETA: a mix of the two

CHRONOSCOPE: a joint label with NESS

Launched in 2023 and now three releases deep, Chronoscope (TGPXLDG) is a joint project between LDG and The Gods Planet, the label run by Italy's NESS. The structure is two tracks from the invited artist on the A-side and a NESS and YUTA project on the B-side, with the focus firmly on vinyl.

YUTA handles curation and promotion. Building the overall image and the initial ideas also come from him. Distribution and coordinating with the pressing plant fall to NESS, who has worked as a studio engineer and managed a vinyl shop.

EDEN & SPECTACLE

Bringing art into techno.

LDG has two physical arenas: "EDEN," the outdoor festival held repeatedly at Kawaba Ski Resort in Gunma, and "SPECTACLE," started together with DJ Nobu.

  1. 2020.08Teams up with Katharsis for an outdoor party at a campsite in Katsuura, Chiba
  2. 2021.07Holds the first "EDEN," billed as the label's 5th anniversary, at Kawaba Ski Resort
  3. 2021Launches "SPECTACLE" with DJ Nobu
  4. 2022EDEN welcomes international acts including Psyk, Tobias., NESS, Feral and Mike Parker
  5. 2024EDEN stages a 25-hour non-stop marathon rave
  6. 2025.10SPECTACLE unveils its current AV show format
  7. 2026.03SPECTACLE invites Byetone from Raster Media
  8. 2027EDEN in preparation for its first edition in three years

The 2020 outdoor party took place while the pandemic was still underway, with the dates chosen around the phased lifting of restrictions. The first EDEN featured domestic artists spanning techno to house, including DJ Nobu, Wata Igarashi, Kuniyuki, OCCA and DJ YAZI, while in 2024 Konduku, Dorisburg, Tauceti, Zemog, DJ MARIA., DOLTZ and Akuma no Numa connected 25 hours together. Since then he has stepped back from the outdoors for a while, shifting the centre of gravity to music production as a label.

When it began, SPECTACLE was a straightforward large-scale techno party. Even at the 2023 edition there was still no AV show element. At the root of the concept is the idea of bringing art into techno. At LDG's outdoor festivals, production elements learned from trance — decor, visuals, lasers — have long been carried over into a techno format.

AV shows tend to lean towards experimental music, but with SPECTACLE he has pushed the dance music element as hard as possible. And now, that idea is heading outdoors. The direction is to concentrate everything the label has built up — the ideas and the track record — into EDEN.

At the end of the interview, YUTA added this for readers.
